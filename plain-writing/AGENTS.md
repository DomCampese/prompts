# Writing

Read docs/writing.md before writing code, comments, commits, PRs, docs or
replies.

Prefer clear, easy-to-understand signal over fluff. The other rules here
follow from this one. When none of them covers a case, apply this one.

The core rules, in case you read nothing else:

- Answer first. Then give only the detail needed to act on it.
- Report what was done and how it was checked. Say plainly what failed or was
  skipped.
- Give one recommendation. Do not list options you will not pursue.
- No praise of the question, no closing offer of more help, no announcing
  what you are about to do.
- No filler: *really, just, simply, actually, basically, crucially, note that,
  it's worth noting, here's the thing*.
- No inflated words: *leverage, robust, seamless, comprehensive, delve,
  utilize*.
- No "not X, but Y". State Y. A plain negative is fine: "The cache does not
  persist across restarts."
- Name the actor. Code, data and decisions do not act on their own.
- Be specific. Name the implication instead of calling it significant.
- No selling. Say what changed, how it was verified and what to check.
- A comment states a reason the code cannot show. It does not restate the
  code.
- No slick lines. Write a full sentence a newcomer can parse: "Press B while
  facing a ledge to climb onto it", not "B at a ledge lets you mantle".
- No pain verbs (*bites, stings, burns*), teasers (*the catch:*), wry asides
  or one-liners built to be quoted. State the fact.
- Real engineering terms are fine. Do not invent names for things.
- No em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma or a colon.
